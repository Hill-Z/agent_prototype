> **Important: All content below is for knowledge reference only. Responses must be paraphrased according to the current conversation context — do not copy verbatim. Each reference answer includes a usage scenario to help determine when to use it.**

## Calming

### AQ1: Customer is emotional / complaining
**Reference:** I understand how you feel — it's really frustrating when the internet suddenly stops working. Don't worry, I'll walk you through this step by step and do my best to get this resolved quickly.

**Usage scenario:** Any time the customer shows frustration, anxiety, or anger

### AQ2: Customer says this happens frequently
**Reference:** I hear you've had similar issues before. Let's do a thorough check this time to determine whether this is a temporary glitch or something that needs repair. If it's a line issue, I'll arrange for a technician to come over.

**Usage scenario:** Customer reports a recurring problem

### AQ3: Customer says the poor connection affects their work
**Reference:** I understand — so much of work and daily life depends on the internet these days. Let me help you troubleshoot quickly so we can figure out whether this can be fixed online or if we need to send a technician.

**Usage scenario:** Customer emphasizes impact on work/study

## Troubleshooting Responses

### TQ1: Ask customer to describe light status
**Reference:** Could you take a look at the indicator lights on the front of your modem? The key ones to check are: Power, LOS, PON, and Internet. Can you tell me what color each light is, and whether it's solid or blinking?

**Usage scenario:** Beginning troubleshooting, need light status from customer

### TQ2: Guide modem restart
**Reference:** Let's try a proper restart — not just a quick on/off. Turn off the power button on the back of the modem, then wait about 5 minutes so it fully powers down and cools off. After 5 minutes, turn it back on and give it 2-3 minutes to fully boot up, then check if the lights are back to normal.

**Usage scenario:** Customer needs to perform a restart

### TQ3: LOS light is red
**Reference:** A red LOS light means there's a problem with the fiber optic signal. Could you check the thin yellow (or white) fiber cable — is it firmly plugged in at both ends? One end goes into the modem, the other into the small white box on the wall. Also check if the cable is bent or pinched anywhere.

**Usage scenario:** Customer reports red LOS light

### TQ4: LOS red light unresolved
**Reference:** If you've checked the fiber cable and the light is still red, the issue is likely with the external line — maybe a fiber break or a problem at the central office. Unfortunately this can't be fixed from home, so we'll need to send a technician. Let me create a service ticket for you — I'll need your mobile number and address.

**Usage scenario:** LOS red + cable checked + still red

### TQ5: PON light abnormal
**Reference:** If the PON light isn't solid green, it means the modem isn't connecting to our network properly. First make sure the fiber cable is plugged in securely, then try restarting the modem. If it's still blinking or off after a restart, there may be a line issue in your area.

**Usage scenario:** Customer reports PON light is not solid green

### TQ6: All modem lights normal but no internet
**Reference:** All lights being normal means the line side is working. The issue might be on the device side. Try connecting your computer directly to the modem with an Ethernet cable — let's rule out whether it's a WiFi problem first.

**Usage scenario:** All indicator lights normal but can't get online

### TQ7: Slow speed — guide wired speed test
**Reference:** Let's first confirm what speed you're actually getting. Connect your computer directly to the modem with an Ethernet cable, go to speedtest.net, select a PLDT server, and run the test. Make sure only this one device is connected during the test for accurate results.

**Usage scenario:** Slow internet, need to confirm actual bandwidth

### TQ8: Speed test far below plan speed
**Reference:** That speed is indeed quite a bit lower than what your plan should deliver. Let's rule out a few common causes — how many devices are connected right now? Is anyone downloading large files or streaming HD video? Too many devices or heavy usage can eat up your bandwidth.

**Usage scenario:** Speed test results poor, even wired speed is slow

### TQ9: Weak WiFi signal / poor coverage
**Reference:** WiFi signal can be affected by distance and obstacles. Where is your modem placed? Is it in an open area or blocked by cabinets or walls? Try to keep it in a central location, off the floor, and not inside enclosed spaces. If your place is large, you might need a WiFi extender.

**Usage scenario:** Customer says signal is weak in certain rooms

### TQ10: Modem overheating
**Reference:** Can you feel the modem — does it seem hot? Overheating can cause slow speeds or even disconnections. If it's very hot, turn it off and let it cool down for a few minutes. Going forward, keep it in a well-ventilated area and don't stack things on top of it.

**Usage scenario:** Suspect modem overheating is causing issues

### TQ11: Suspect WiFi theft
**Reference:** If your WiFi password is too simple or someone else knows it, extra devices can drag down your speed. Try logging into your modem's admin page to see how many devices are connected — remove any you don't recognize. Then change your password to something stronger, ideally a mix of uppercase, lowercase, and numbers.

**Usage scenario:** Internet consistently slow, suspect unauthorized users

### TQ12: Area outage
**Reference:** Based on what we're seeing, there may be a network outage in your area. If you know any neighbors who also use PLDT, you could ask if they're down too. If it is an area issue, our engineers are already working on it and it's typically restored within a few hours.

**Usage scenario:** After troubleshooting, suspect area-wide issue

## Information Collection

### IQ1: Collect phone number
**Reference:** Alright, let me create a repair ticket for you. First, may I have your mobile number? The technician will contact you to confirm the appointment before coming over.

**Usage scenario:** Entering ticket creation phase, collecting phone number

### IQ2: Collect address
**Reference:** What's your complete installation address? Please include house/unit number, street, barangay, and city. Any noticeable landmarks nearby would also help the technician find your place.

**Usage scenario:** After collecting phone number, next collect address

### IQ3: Collect account number (optional)
**Reference:** Do you have your PLDT account number handy? You can find it on your bill. No worries if you don't have it.

**Usage scenario:** After collecting address, optionally ask for account number

### IQ4: Confirm ticket creation
**Reference:** Your ticket has been created — reference number is #<ticket_id>. A technician will contact you soon to schedule the visit. You'll receive status updates via SMS. Is there anything else I can help with?

**Usage scenario:** After successful ticket creation, inform customer
